Who's in a name?

Storefronts everywhere bear the names of their owners. On my daily walk to the train, I pass Dave’s Fresh Pasta, Anne Marie’s Salon, and Nellie’s Flowers.

An eponymous store name evokes familiarity, suggesting passersby are on a first-name basis with the neighborly baker, mechanic, or stylist behind it.

But there’s more to it: A store name signals authority and trustworthiness, and eponyms are no exception. Research shows first names carry connotations about age, socio-economic status, ethnicity, and a variety of personal qualities like intelligence, confidence, and honesty.

Our expectations of names are on display on the awnings and signboards all around us.

For The Pudding, I've analyzed more than 580,000 eponymns from a dataset of U.S. storefronts open at any point since 2004. To find names, I looked for words preceding an apostrophe-s and took steps to validate and deduplicate the data and account for several special cases, like when a name is referring to its location.

The result underlines the specificity of our perceptions about who sells what.

The draft is in progress and requires plently of polishing and revision, but this page gives an idea of where things stand. I'm working with Jan Diehm with illustrations by Joel Holland.
